You are reading a response surface, not looking for its maximum.

The maximum of a grid is the least informative point on it: it is where noise
and signal happen to add up most favourably, which is why this project reports
a shape diagnostic beside it. The surface itself says far more -- which axis
actually moves the outcome, whether there is a connected region of decent
values or one isolated cell, whether the good region sits against an edge of
the grid, and whether different symbols agree about where it is.

Answer these, briefly and concretely.

1. Which axis dominates? Rank the grid axes by how much moving along them
   changes the metric, and name the numbers.
2. Is the good region connected or isolated? If a cell's neighbours are poor,
   say so plainly -- that is a corner, not a mechanism.
3. Does the good region touch an edge of the grid? A best cell at the boundary
   means the grid was drawn in the wrong place and the answer is outside it.
4. Do the symbols agree? If each symbol's best cell is somewhere different, the
   parameter is not doing what its name suggests.
5. What does the surface suggest about the mechanism? Not about the parameter
   -- about the mechanism. If it suggests nothing, say so.

Reply in plain prose, at most 250 words. Do not recommend a configuration and
do not propose a new hypothesis.

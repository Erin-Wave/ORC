## What the evaluator can express

A hypothesis is only runnable if every key in `grid` and `fixed` is a field of
the config type its track uses. Anything else expands to a TypeError and the
proposal is killed mechanically before it reaches the queue. Propose within
this vocabulary, or propose a rule shape that fits it.

Track A -- TrialConfig fields:
  contribution, stride_days, n_contributions, hold_days, leverage, gate, take_profit, stop_loss, include_funding, clock, fee_bps, slippage_bps, cost_multiplier
  `gate` is a string: "none", "dip:<drop>:<lookback_days>", or "sma:<days>".

Track B -- SignalTrialConfig fields:
  rule, lookback_days, enter_rate, exit_rate, timeframe_hours, enter_level, exit_level, filter_timeframe_hours, filter_lookback_days, filter_level, oi_drop, capital, leverage, stop_loss, take_profit, max_hold_days, clock, fee_bps, slippage_bps, cost_multiplier
  `rule` must be one of: carry_funding, carry_funding_long, cci_breakout, cci_mtf, cci_reversion, oi_confirmed_reversion

  The carry rules read the funding series and use `enter_rate` / `exit_rate`,
  which are per SETTLEMENT and not annualised.

  The three cci rules read PRICE and use `enter_level` / `exit_level`, in the
  units the literature quotes: +-100 is the band, +-200 the extreme.
  `enter_level` must be above `exit_level` -- the gap is the rule's hysteresis.
  `lookback_days` is the CCI period expressed as a DURATION, so one registered
  value means the same window on every timeframe. `timeframe_hours` is the
  candle the indicator is read on, aggregated from the execution clock: 4.0
  with clock "1h" is the 4-hour candle traded on the hourly bar, and 1.0 is the
  bar itself. `cci_mtf` additionally needs `filter_timeframe_hours` (STRICTLY
  slower than `timeframe_hours`), `filter_level` (must be positive), and
  optionally `filter_lookback_days`; the slow candle decides which side may be
  taken and the fast one triggers a pullback entry inside it.

  `oi_confirmed_reversion` is new on 2026-09-05 and it is the first rule in
  this project that reads something other than price and funding. It is
  `cci_reversion` with one extra condition: open interest must have FALLEN by
  at least `oi_drop` (a positive fraction) over the indicator's own window for
  a bar to be admissible. It needs `oi_drop` and refuses without it.

  Why that is a different test and not a finer grid. Section 7b says the CCI
  family is a PARTIAL test because a displaced price is evidence of forced flow
  rather than a measurement of it, and ordinary aggressive trading displaces
  price too. Open interest separates them in ONE direction: a forced close
  reduces it and a new position raises it, so a move on which open interest
  ROSE cannot be a liquidation cascade whatever the price did. That is
  refutation, not confirmation, and it is the whole of what the rule adds.

  So it is a PAIR with unconditional `cci_reversion`, the way reversion and
  breakout are a pair. If the unconditional rule pays and this one does not,
  what the unconditional rule reads is not forced closing. Say which of those
  two the claim is about.

  Measured before any of it was registered: on the median symbol open interest
  fell on 61% of the worst 1% of hourly returns against a 50% baseline. So a
  binding `oi_drop` should remove a noticeable share of entries, and a level
  that removes almost none has found a threshold that is not doing anything.

  It is still not the liquidation stream, and a fall in open interest does not
  prove the closes were compulsory -- a voluntary exit reduces it identically.
  A claim that says more than "this bar was not a new-position move" is
  claiming something the data did not measure.

  Positioning is held for the nine researched symbols only, at 5-minute
  resolution, from 2021-12 (BTCUSDT from 2020-09). A universe naming a symbol
  without it records the skip rather than a number.
